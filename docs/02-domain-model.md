# Domain model & Firestore schema

The canonical definitions are the zod schemas in `libs/domain`. This page is the map. Every collection below lives under `orgs/{orgId}` unless marked **global** or **user**.

Common fields, omitted below, are on every document: `createdAt`, `createdBy`, `updatedAt`, `updatedBy`, `schemaVersion`. Soft-deletable documents also have `deletedAt?` and `deletedBy?` (`null` means live, like a missing field; live matters always store `deletedAt: null` so lists can filter on it).

Types used below: `DateOnly` = `YYYY-MM-DD`; `Cents` = integer AUD cents; `Minutes` = integer.

## Tenancy & people

## Contacts

`orgs/{orgId}/contacts/{contactId}`:
- `kind: 'person'|'organisation'`
- Person fields: `givenNames`, `familyName`, `preferredName?`, `dateOfBirth?: DateOnly`, `dateOfDeath?: DateOnly`
- Organisation fields: `orgName`, `abn?`
- `emails[]`, `phones[]` (E.164; legacy `04…` becomes `+614…`), `addresses[] {type, line1, line2?, suburb, state, postcode, country:'AU'}`
- `tags[]`, `isReferrer: bool`, `referrerMeta?: {company?, industry?: 'accountant'|'financial_planner'|'funeral_director'|'law_firm'|'aged_care'|'bank'|'other'}` (CORE-18; free text stored before the enum reads as `other`, and `referrerIndustryFromText` maps legacy text), `notes?`
- `search: string[]` (prefix tokens), `dedupeKey` (normalised name plus DOB), `sortName` (lower-cased display name, for `orderBy`): server-owned, kept current by the `contacts-onWrite` trigger
- `mergedInto?`, used when contacts are merged; it is a tombstone pointer. A tombstone is also soft-deleted and the rules freeze it.

Functions (CORE-01): `contacts-create` (`contact.edit`; creates are callable-only), `contacts-findDuplicates` (`contact.edit`; best five live contacts at or above `DUPLICATE_THRESHOLD`), `contacts-merge` (`contact.merge`; folds one contact into another, re-points parties, matter client/referrer/deceased/testator ids and safe custody packets, 200 in the call and the rest through the `contacts-continueMerge` queue; audited as `contact.merged`) and `contacts-delete` (`contact.delete`; soft delete, refused with `FAILED_PRECONDITION`, `details.reason: 'contact_referenced'` and `details.references: ContactReference[]` while anything references the contact).

## Matters

`orgs/{orgId}/matters/{matterId}`:

**Identity and type**
- `number` (unique per org), `title`.
- `type: 'will'|'poa'|'probate'|'loa'|'admin'|'fp'|'contest'|'trust'` (the `MatterTag` tokens).
- `status: 'lead'|'open'|'on_hold'|'closed'|'archived'`.
- `stageId` (from the matter template), `stageEnteredOn?: DateOnly` (server-owned; the day, org time zone, the matter entered the stage; template task due dates count from it, CORE-07), `templateId` (`system-{type}` or an org template id), `templateVersion` (the published version the matter follows; it never changes under the matter, CORE-07).
- `jurisdiction`.

**People**
- `responsibleUid`, `teamUids[]`, `clientContactIds[]`.
- `deceased?: {contactId, dateOfDeath?: DateOnly, domicile, lastAddress}`. `dateOfDeath` is absent while unknown (CORE-05): no `date_of_death` event, template tasks anchored on it show "due date pending", and intake creates a death certificate task; recording it later (key facts) makes the event known.
- `testator?: {contactId}`, for will and POA matters (for a POA, the principal).
- `facts?: {willDate?: DateOnly, courtFileNo?, trustName?}`: key facts not held elsewhere (CORE-06; `trustName` for trust matters, CORE-05).
- `referrerContactId?` or, when no contact referred the matter, `referralSource?: 'unknown'|'website'|'existing_client'|'walk_in'` (CORE-18; never both). Set at intake (clients step) and edited on the matter header (client-direct). A referrer's contact page lists the live matters with their `referrerContactId` (equality filters only, no composite index; `privileged == false` without `privileged.view`), with the count, the active count and the last referral date; billed fees join with BILL-07.

**Fees and conflicts**
- `fee: {arrangement: 'hourly'|'fixed'|'capped'|'conditional'|'pro_bono', amountCents?, capCents?, estimateCents?, rateOverrideCents?, conditional?: {upliftPct (≤ 25), condition}, history[]}`. `FeeInput` (CORE-05) checks the arrangement: fixed needs `amountCents`, capped `capCents` (and an estimate within it), conditional `conditional`, pro bono no amounts.
- `costsDisclosure: {status: 'not_required'|'required'|'sent'|'updated'|'waived', sentOn?: DateOnly, estimateCents?, documentId?}`. At intake the status comes from the fee's estimate (`feeEstimateCents`: the estimate, else the fixed fee or cap, 0 for pro bono) through `costsDisclosureRequirement`, a **placeholder** single threshold (`COSTS_DISCLOSURE_PLACEHOLDER_THRESHOLD_CENTS`, $750) until BILL-06 replaces it; an unknown estimate needs a disclosure.
- `conflictCheck: {status: 'pending'|'clear'|'potential'|'conflict', checkedAt, checkedBy, resultId, reason?}`. `resultId` names the `conflictChecks` run the determination was made on; `reason` is required for `potential` and `conflict` (CORE-02).
- `conflictAlert?: {checkId, contactId, partyId?, at, adverse}`: server-owned, set by `conflicts-onPartyAdded` when a party added to an open or on-hold matter is on other matters; cleared by `conflicts-determine` on the same check. It never blocks anything (CORE-02).

**Estate and dispute summaries**
- `estate?: {grossValueCents?, netValueCents?, grantType?: 'probate'|'loa'|'loa_with_will'|'reseal', grantDate?: DateOnly, grantNo?}`.
- `dispute?: {kind: 'family_provision'|'will_contest'|'construction'|'executor_removal'|'other', proceedingIds[]}`.

**Housekeeping and denormalised fields**
- `privileged: bool`, `priority: 'none'|'urgent'|'high'|'medium'|'low'`.
- `externalRef?` (the matter's number in another system), `externalUrl?` (a link to the matter elsewhere, e.g. 3E: an absolute http(s) URL, client-updatable, CORE-21a), `boardOrder?: number` (the Matters board's card order, CORE-04: a fractional index written by the client as the midpoint of its neighbours; not activity, not audited).
- `labels[]`, `relatedMatterIds[]`.
- `closedAt?`, `closeReason?`, `destroyAfter?: DateOnly`.
- `search[]`.
- Denormalised for lists: `nextDeadline?: {id, dueDate, title, critical}`, `openTaskCount`, `wipCents`, `lastActivityAt`.

Clients update only `title`, `responsibleUid`, `teamUids`, `labels`, `priority`, `externalRef`, `estate`, `deceased`, `facts` and `privileged` (the flag also needs `privileged.view`), and soft delete (`matter.delete`). Everything else goes through functions (CORE-03, CORE-06):

- `matters-createMatter` (`matter.create`, idempotent): allocates the number in a transaction from `counters/matter-{YYYY}` (the year in the org's time zone, so 1 January in Melbourne) and `matterNumberFormat` (default `M-{YYYY}-{seq:4}`), skipping any number already reserved in `matterNumbers/{matterNumberKey(number)}` or held by an existing matter, so a format change mid-year or a migrated matter can't cause a duplicate. Writes the counter, the reservation, the matter (`lead`, or `open` when `conflictCheck.status` is `clear` or `potential`), `parties` rows for the clients, deceased and testator, and a `matter.created` audit entry. With the `templates.system` flag on (CORE-07) the same transaction gives the matter a template version (the input's `templateId`/`templateVersion` when given, else the org's most recently published template of the type, else the type's system template), its first stage (or the input's stage when the template has it) and that stage's template tasks plus later ones whose anchor is known (see Matter templates); with it off, `templateId` is only stored. A migrated matter keeps `externalRef` and gets a new number. A `conflictCheck.status` other than `pending` is a determination at intake (CORE-02): it needs `conflict.determine` (`PERMISSION_DENIED`, `details.reason: 'conflict_determine_required'`), a reason for `potential` and `conflict`, and a `resultId` (when given) naming a check run for a new matter; the server stamps `checkedAt` and `checkedBy` and audits `matter.conflict_determined`.
- `matters-renumber` (`matter.renumber`: lawyer and above, idempotent, CORE-21a): gives a live matter a typed number (at most 40 characters). Refuses a number another matter has reserved or holds, case-insensitively (`ALREADY_EXISTS`); moves the `matterNumbers` reservation (the old one is released, so createMatter can reuse it) and updates `number` and `search` in one transaction. Audited as `matter.renumbered`. `{TYPE}` in the format comes from `org.matterTypeCodes`, else the defaults (`WIL`, `POA`, `PRB`, `LOA`, `ADM`, `FP`, `CON`, `TRU`).
- `matters-rebalanceBoard` (`matter.edit`, idempotent, CORE-04): when a board column's `boardOrder`s run out of precision (or some cards were never placed), writes evenly spaced orders (`1024` apart) for up to 500 matters in the order given, in one batch. Writes only `boardOrder`; missing, deleted and (without `privileged.view`) privileged matters are skipped.
  - **Intake (CORE-05).** The input also takes, all optional and backward compatible: `parties: [{contactId, role, share?, notes?}]` (≤ 100), `events: [{kind, date, label?}]` (≤ 20), `fee` (`FeeInput`, wins over `matter.fee`; one of the two is required), `costsDisclosure: {status: 'required'|'not_required'|'waived', estimateCents?}` (wins over `matter.costsDisclosure`; computed from the fee when neither is given; a `not_required` the estimate doesn't allow is `INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `costsDisclosure.status`) and `conflictCheckId` (stored as `conflictCheck.resultId`; it must agree with `matter.conflictCheck.resultId` when both are given). `matter.fee` and `matter.costsDisclosure` became optional in the input. In the same transaction as the matter it writes every party row (`initialParties` plus `parties`, one per `partyDocId(role, contactId)`, so duplicates collapse and a testator who is also an executor is two rows), the events (deduplicated, plus a `date_of_death` event from `deceased.dateOfDeath` when known; a listed one must match it), the template's stage and tasks (seeing those events as anchors), and, when the deceased's date of death is unknown, exactly one death certificate task at `tasks/tpl-obtain_death_certificate` (`templateTaskKey: obtain_death_certificate`): the probate and LOA templates already have it, otherwise it is added (`source: 'manual'`), and a later template apply never adds a second. After the transaction it recomputes the new matter's deadlines (CORE-10, cause `matter_created`; best effort: a failure is logged and the next recompute catches up). A known date of death after the org's today, or before the deceased contact's `dateOfBirth`, is `INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `matter.deceased.dateOfDeath`. Party contacts are read in the transaction; a missing, deleted or merged one is `INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `parties.{i}.contactId` and nothing is written (counter, reservation, matter, rows). A `conflict` determination creates a lead. The `matter.created` audit adds `parties`, `events` and `deathCertificateTask`.
  - **Step schemas** (`libs/domain/src/lib/intake.ts`), shared by the New matter form and the tests: `IntakeTypeStep` (`type`, `jurisdiction` = VIC), `IntakeClientsStep` (`clientContactIds`, ≥ 1, unique; new contacts go through `contacts-create` first), `IntakeDetailsStep`/`intakeDetailsStep({today})` (by type: will `testatorContactId`; poa `principalContactId`; probate/loa/admin `deceasedContactId`, `deceasedDateOfBirth?` (checked against only), `dateOfDeath: DateOnly|'unknown'`, `domicile`, `lastAddress?`, `willExists`, `willDate?`; fp/contest the deceased, `domicile?`, `ourRole: 'plaintiff'|'defendant'|'executor'` and `otherSideContactIds` (≥ 1, put in `OTHER_SIDE_ROLE`); trust `trustName`, `trusteeContactIds`), `IntakePartiesStep` (`parties`, no repeated role and contact), `IntakeFeesStep` (`fee`, `costsDisclosureWaived`), `IntakeConflictStep` (`checkId`, `status`, `reason` for potential and conflict) and `IntakeReviewStep` (`title`, `responsibleUid`, `teamUids`, `labels`, `priority`, `privileged`). `intakeFormSchema({today})` checks the whole form (details for the chosen type, no client on the other side) and `intakeToCreateMatterInput(form, requestId)` builds the callable input. Date checks: a date of death in the future or before the date of birth, a date of birth in the future, a will dated in the future or after the death, a will date without a will, and probate without a will are errors. The form's "Prefill from email or notes" button stays disabled behind the `clerk.features.intakePrefill` flag (default off, client-only for now).
- `matters-changeStatus` (`matter.status`): `MATTER_TRANSITIONS` (lead → open; open → on hold or closed; on hold → open; closed → open or archived), else `MATTER_INVALID_TRANSITION`. Lead → open needs a conflict determination of `clear` or `potential` (`CONFLICT_OPENABLE`); `pending` and `conflict` are `FAILED_PRECONDITION` with `details.reason: 'conflict_check_not_clear'` and `details.conflictStatus` (CORE-02); closed → open needs a `reason` (`INVALID_ARGUMENT`). Closing sets `closedAt` and `closeReason`; reopening clears them. Audited as `matter.status_changed`.
- `matters-changeStage` (`matter.edit`, idempotent; CORE-06, CORE-07): moves a live, visible matter that is not closed or archived (`FAILED_PRECONDITION`, `details.reason: 'matter_closed'`) to one of its stages: its template version's (the system bundle, `templates/{templateId}/versions/{templateVersion}`, else `templates/{templateId}.stages`) when it has one, else the type's defaults (`matterStages`). An unknown stage is `INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `stageId`; the same stage is a no-op. With `templates.system` off it is CORE-06: a forward move needs the key facts every stage passed into requires, else `MATTER_STAGE_REQUIREMENTS` with `details.requirements` (key fact ids), and moving back is always allowed. With it on, a forward move (skips included) needs, for every stage passed into, its key facts (`entryRequires.facts` plus those of the CORE-06 default stage with the same id), known events (`entryRequires.events`) and done template tasks (`entryRequires.tasksDone`), else `MATTER_STAGE_REQUIREMENTS` with `details.requirements` (key fact ids, as before), `details.events` (event kinds) and `details.tasks` (template task keys). Moving back needs `matter.status` (`PERMISSION_DENIED`, `details.reason: 'stage_back_requires_lawyer'`) and a `reason` (`INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `reason`); later stages' tasks stay. Entering a stage creates its template tasks and sets `stageEnteredOn`; the result lists `createdTaskIds`. Audited as `matter.stage_changed` (`from`, `to`, and `direction: 'back'` with the `reason` going back).
- `matters-upgradeTemplate` (`matter.edit`, idempotent, flag `templates.system`; CORE-07): `{matterId, version}` moves a live, open matter to that (same or later) published version of its template. It adds only the missing template tasks of the current stage (plus later ones whose anchor is known); earlier stages never get new tasks and nothing is deleted; future stages get the new version's tasks when entered. A stage the new version dropped follows the versions' `stageMapping`, else `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (`details.reason: 'stage_unmapped'`). No template: `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (`'no_template'`); an earlier version: `INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `version`; an unknown one: `NOT_FOUND`. Audited as `matter.template_upgraded`.
- `matters-upsertParty` and `matters-removeParty` (`matter.edit`, idempotent, privileged matters for `privileged.view` roles only; CORE-06): party rows live at `parties/{partyDocId(role, contactId)}`. Upsert without `partyId` adds a live, unmerged contact (else `INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `contactId`) in a role: a new row, a revived soft-deleted row, or an edit of the live one; with `partyId` it edits that row, and a changed role or contact soft-deletes it and creates or revives the row at the new id. `share` and `notes` are the row's full state. Remove soft-deletes the row. Both recompute the matter's `clientContactIds` from the live client rows (`clientContactIdsFrom`; a listed client with no client row at all, from before CORE-06, is kept) and refuse an empty result with `LAST_CLIENT`; `matters-denormalise` then refreshes `search`. Audited as `matter.party_added`, `matter.party_updated` and `matter.party_removed` (ids and roles only). Clients edit a row's `share` and `notes` directly, nothing else.
- `matters-restore` (`matter.restore`: owner, admin): restores a soft-deleted matter within 30 days of its delete, else `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (`details.reason: 'restore_window_passed'`). The rules freeze a deleted matter and never let a client restore it.
- `conflicts-run` (`conflict.run`: paralegal and above, idempotent): scores each subject (`ConflictSubject`) against the org's contacts with the CORE-01 duplicate scorer (field-mask scan), skipping deleted contacts and reporting a hit on a merge tombstone as the contact it was merged into (`viaMergedContactId`). The subject's own `contactId` is reported as `self` (score 1) when it is on other matters. For each hit, the `parties` collection group lists every matter it is on, closed, archived and deleted ones included (`deleted: true`), `excludeMatterId` left out; privileged matters show `PRIVILEGED_MATTER_TITLE`. With the `conflicts.enforced` flag on, a role adverse to the subject's (`ADVERSE_ROLE_PAIRS`) carries `adverse` (the pair's label). Stored as `conflictChecks/{id}`; never changes a matter.
- `conflicts-determine` (`conflict.determine`: lawyer and above, idempotent): writes `matter.conflictCheck = {status, checkedAt, checkedBy, resultId, reason}` from a check run for that matter (or before it existed), clears a `conflictAlert` from the same check, and audits `matter.conflict_determined` with the reason. A check for another matter is `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (`details.reason: 'check_for_another_matter'`).
- `conflicts-onPartyAdded` (trigger on `parties` create) and `conflicts-onPartyRevived` (on update, for a soft-deleted row made live again, CORE-06): for an open or on-hold matter (not leads, and not the rows `matters-createMatter` writes with the matter), re-checks the party's contact in its role, stores the run, and when it is on other matters sets `conflictAlert` and sends the responsible lawyer a high-priority `conflict_alert` notification linking to `/matters/{id}`.
- `matters-denormalise` (trigger): keeps `search` (number, title, client and deceased names, external reference), `lastActivityAt` (moved forward on any change outside the denormalised fields) and `deletedAt: null` current, writing only stale fields so it never loops. `openTaskCount` is kept by `tasks-onWrite` (CORE-08, see Tasks), `nextDeadline` by `deadlines-onWrite` (CORE-10, see Deadlines) and `wipCents` by `time-onWrite` (BILL-01, see Time entries).

#### Matters list queries

The list queries `orgs/{orgId}/matters` with `deletedAt == null`, plus `privileged == false` for roles without `privileged.view`, the filters below, and cursor pagination of 50. `MATTER_INDEXED_QUERIES` in `libs/domain` (with `isIndexedMatterQuery`) is the same list; any other combination, or a sort on client, responsible, stage, next deadline or WIP, should query the closest indexed subset and filter or sort the rest client-side. `status` and `type` take `==` or `in` (the product of `in` list sizes is at most 30); `labels` and `search` take `array-contains` (one per query). Each row is indexed with and without `privileged`; `firebase/tests/indexes.spec.ts` checks the file matches.

### Matter templates (CORE-07)

A template is a matter type's stages, the tasks each stage creates and the deadline rules that apply (`libs/domain/src/lib/templates.ts`). It sits behind the `templates.system` flag (server-enforced, off by default, on for the demo org): with it off, matters behave as in CORE-06 (default stages, no tasks) and `templates-*` and `matters-upgradeTemplate` return `FEATURE_DISABLED`.

- **Shape.** `stages[] {id, name, order, criteria?, entryRequires? {facts?: KeyFactId[], events?: EventKind[], tasksDone?: taskKey[]}, terminal?}` in increasing `order`; the first stage has no requirements, and `tasksDone` names tasks of earlier stages. `tasks[] {key, title, stageId, assignTo: 'responsible'\|'role:paralegal'\|null, due?: {fromEvent: EventKind, offsetDays} \| {fromStageEntry: days}, priority, checklist?: string[]}`. `deadlineRuleIds[]` names CORE-09 rules (`vic-v1`); statutory deadlines are never template tasks. `TemplateContentSchema` validates a template, and `templateProblems` lists what's wrong.
- **System templates** ship with the code, one per matter type (`libs/domain/templates/system/{type}.json`, id `system-{type}`, version 1), and a unit test validates them against the schema and the rule set. They are not seeded into Firestore. Practitioner sign-off is CORE-07a.
- **Org templates.** `templates-clone` copies a system template or an org template's version into a new draft, `templates-save` edits the draft, and `templates-publish` copies it to `versions/{n + 1}`. Dropping a stage that a live matter on the template is in needs a `stageMapping` (`TEMPLATE_STAGE_MAPPING_REQUIRED` otherwise). A new matter takes the org's most recently published template of its type, else the system one.
- **Tasks.** Applying a template (at create, `templates-apply`, an upgrade) creates the current stage's tasks plus later ones whose anchor event is known; entering a stage creates that stage's. Task ids are `tpl-{key}`, so a retry never duplicates a task and a deleted one is never recreated. `responsible` goes to `matter.responsibleUid`; `role:paralegal` to the first active paralegal (by uid), else the responsible person; `null` stays unassigned. Each created task adds to `openTaskCount`.
- **Due dates** use DateOnly maths. `fromEvent` counts from the earliest live matter event of that kind, else from the key fact standing for it (`date_of_death` ← `deceased.dateOfDeath`, `grant_issued` ← `estate.grantDate`); an unknown anchor leaves `dueDate` unset and sets `duePending` to the event kind. `fromStageEntry` counts from `stageEnteredOn` (today in the org time zone when the stage is entered). A pending due date is not yet filled in when its anchor is recorded later.
- **Screens.** Settings → Templates (`/settings/templates`, owners and admins, behind the flag) lists the system templates (Customise clones one) and the org's templates with their published version; the editor (`/settings/templates/{id}`) edits the draft, checks it with `templateProblems` before `templates-save`, shows server field errors at their paths, and publishes, asking for a stage mapping on `TEMPLATE_STAGE_MAPPING_REQUIRED`. On the matter screen, a refused move lists missing key facts, events and template tasks by name; moving back asks for a reason and is locked for roles without `matter.status`; the "…" menu offers "Upgrade matter to vN" when the org has a newer published version; and open tasks with `duePending` say "Due date pending: <event> unknown".

### Tasks (CORE-08)

Matter tasks (`matters/{matterId}/tasks`) and personal tasks (`members/{uid}/tasks`) share one shape (`Task`, `PersonalTask`) and the `tasks` collection group; personal tasks have `matterId: null` and are always assigned to their owner. Helpers live in `libs/domain/src/lib/tasks.ts`; the quick-add parser is the separate entry point `@materia/domain/quick-add` (it brings chrono-node, so only lazily loaded routes import it).

- **Client-direct writes.** Clients create manual tasks (`MatterTaskCreateInput`: `orgId` and `matterId` matching the path, `source: 'manual'`, `countedOpen: false`, title ≤ 500 characters, checklist ≤ 100 items, group ≤ 120), edit the whitelist (`title`, `status`, `priority`, `assigneeUid`, `dueDate`, `checklist`, `order`, `group`; the rules check the values) and soft delete or restore. `descriptionMd`, `deadlineId` and `source` are set at create; `completedAt`, `countedOpen`, `templateTaskKey` and `duePending` are server-owned. Matter tasks follow the matter's access: `task.edit` roles on a live matter they can read (a privileged matter needs `privileged.view`). Personal tasks are readable and writable only by their owner (not admins). Through the collection group a member reads the tasks assigned to them; only `privileged.view` roles read other matter tasks there (ADR-026).
- **Quick add.** `parseQuickAdd(input, {members, now, timeZone})`: `@name` (a current member, by name, word or initials prefix; ambiguous or unknown mentions stay in the title and are reported), `!urgent|!high|!medium|!low|!none` (and short forms; the last wins) and `due <date>` (chrono-node day-month English, from today's date in the org time zone; a day and month already past is next year's). It returns the title, the fields and the recognised spans.
- **Order.** `order` is fractional: `orderBetween` gives the midpoint of the neighbours (1024 past either end), `planMove` moves one or several tasks with one write each, or rebalances the whole list (1024 apart) when the gap is below 1e-6 or orders tie; `chunkWrites` splits the writes into batches.
- **My tasks.** The group query `orgId == active org, assigneeUid == me, status in open` (no order: undated tasks must come too) returns matter and personal tasks; `sectionMyTasks(tasks, today(org.timezone))` sorts them into Overdue, Today, This week (to Sunday), Later and No date, each by due date, priority and order.
- **Checklist templates (CORE-08d).** "Templates" in a task's details lists the org's `checklistTemplates` (searchable by name) and applies one with `applyChecklistTemplate(task, template, {today})` (`libs/domain/src/lib/checklist-templates.ts`): the template's items the checklist doesn't already have (case-insensitive, trimmed match) are added unticked with new ids, up to `TASK_CHECKLIST_MAX`; the template's `priority` is used only when the task's is `none`, and `dueDate = today(org.timezone) + dueInDays` only when the task has no due date. One client write (`checklist`, plus `priority`/`dueDate`); it reports items added and skipped (already there or over the limit). Per-item dates are out of scope.
- **Task groups and group templates (CORE-08e, CORE-08f).** A task's free-text `group` is set from its details (the Group field) or in bulk (`BulkTaskChange` `{op: 'group', group}`, `null` clears it); Stage view gives named groups that aren't stages their own sections. "Templates" on a matter's Tasks tab lists the org's `taskGroupTemplates` and adds one with `planTaskGroup(template, {today, firstOrder})` (`libs/domain/src/lib/task-group-templates.ts`): one to-do task per template task, in a group named after the template, after the matter's last task (`orderAfterLast`, 1024 apart), with an unticked checklist (new ids), the template's priority (or `none`) and `dueDate = today(org.timezone) + dueInDays`. One client create per task; the toast's Undo soft deletes them. Templates are edited as an outline (`parseTaskGroupOutline` / `taskGroupOutline`): a task per line, checklist items indented beneath it, `!priority` and `in N days|weeks` on a task line. "Save a group as a template" copies a matter group's titles, checklist texts and priorities (`taskGroupTemplateTasks`), not its due dates. Matter tasks only; personal tasks are out of scope.
- **Former members.** `assigneeState(uid, members)` flags an assignee who was removed (or has no member document) as `former_member`: the task stays and shows "Unassigned (former member)" (`FORMER_MEMBER_LABEL`).
- **`tasks-onWrite`** (trigger on matter tasks): in a transaction on the task, reconciles `countedOpen` (whether it is counted) with its open state (live and `todo`, `in_progress` or `blocked`), adding ±1 to the matter's `openTaskCount`; sets `completedAt` when the status becomes `done` and removes it when reopened. It reads the current state, so concurrent and out-of-order events leave the count exact. Writers that count a task themselves (template tasks, the death certificate task, the seed) write `countedOpen: true`; a task without the field predates CORE-08 and counts as it was before the event. It also sends (through `notify()`, in-app and by email) a `task_assigned` notification to a new assignee of a live open task (not self-assignment; not template or migrated tasks at creation; only active members), and the matter's activity (`task.created`, `task.completed`, `task.reopened`, `task.assigned`, `task.unassigned`, `task.deleted`, `task.restored`) with `lastActivityAt`. `tasks-onPersonalWrite` keeps a personal task's `completedAt`.
- **`tasks-bulkUpdate`** (callable, idempotent): `{tasks: [{matterId \| null, taskId}] (≤ 500, unique), change: {op: 'status'\|'assignee'\|'dueDate'\|'priority'\|'delete', …}}` for selections over `TASK_CLIENT_BATCH_MAX` (50). Matter tasks need `task.edit` (`PERMISSION_DENIED`) and a live matter the caller can read; `matterId: null` is the caller's own personal task. A new assignee must be an active member (`INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `change.assigneeUid`); `null` unassigns or clears the date. Writes in batches of 250 with `_requestId`; returns `{updated, skipped: [{matterId, taskId, reason}]}` (`not_found`, `deleted`, `matter_unavailable`, `personal_assignee`, `unchanged`).

### Time entries (BILL-01)

Matter time entries (`matters/{matterId}/timeEntries`) and the `timeEntries` collection group. Helpers live in `libs/domain/src/lib/time-entries.ts`; all the maths is integer.

- **Units and amounts.** `timeUnits(minutes, {unitMinutes = 6, rounding})`: `up` is the ceiling, `nearest` rounds half up. `timeAmountCents(units, rateCents, unitMinutes)` rounds half up to the cent. Durations parse with `parseDuration`: decimal hours (`0.3`, `0.25` = 15 minutes, exact to the minute), minutes (`18m`), hours and minutes (`1h 12m`) and `1:12`; zero, negative and over 100 hours are rejected (`checkDuration`). More than 24 hours on a day for one person asks once before saving.
- **Rates.** `resolveRate({member, matter, date, org})`: the matter's `fee.rateOverrideCents`, else the member's `rates[]` entry in force on the entry's date (the latest `effectiveFrom` on or before it; before the first, the earliest), else the member's `defaultRateCents` (members from before BILL-01), else the org's `billing.defaultRateCents`. Settings → Team saves a change with `withRateChange`, which keeps the rate it replaces from `2000-01-01` the first time, so earlier dates keep their rate. Non-billable entries keep the rate and amount (for write-off reports); fixed-fee and pro bono matters start entries non-billable (`defaultBillable`), shown "Fixed fee".
- **Client-direct writes.** Members with `time.record` create their own drafts on a live matter they can read (`TimeEntryCreateInput`: `status: 'draft'`, `source: 'manual'`, `wipCountedCents: 0`) and edit or soft delete their own drafts (`time.approve` roles: anyone's) through the whitelist `date`, `minutes`, `units`, `rateCents`, `amountCents`, `narrative`, `billable`, `taskId`. The client's units, rate and amount are a preview: the trigger replaces them. Final, billed and written-off entries are never client-writable. Through the group a member reads their own entries; only `privileged.view` roles read other people's.
- **`time-onWrite`** (trigger): for a live draft, recomputes units, rate and amount from the org's billing settings, the matter's fee and the author's rate on the entry's date (never trusting the client). In a transaction on the entry, it reconciles `wipCountedCents` (what it has counted) with the entry's contribution (the billable amount of a live draft or final entry; billed and written off count 0) and adds the difference to the matter's `wipCents`, so concurrent and out-of-order events leave it exact and it never loops. Writers that count an entry themselves (the seed) write `wipCountedCents` equal to the amount. A new entry moves the matter's `lastActivityAt`. Metrics hooks (INS-01) are out of scope.
- **`time-setStatus`** (callable, `time.approve`, idempotent): `{matterId, entryIds (≤ 100), status: 'final'\|'draft'}` approves drafts (snapshotting units, rate and amount once more) or returns final entries to draft; billed, written-off, deleted and missing entries are skipped. Returns `{updated, skipped}`. Billing them (`billed`, `invoiceId`) is BILL-07's.
- **UI.** The matter's Time tab (grouped by day with day and matter totals; an add row, `t`; edit, delete, Mark final and Back to draft from a row's menu; filter by person) and quick entry from anywhere (`t`, or ⌘K "Log time": the open matter, else recent matters first, or search). The narrative field offers a built-in phrase library on `/` (`DEFAULT_NARRATIVE_PHRASES`; a per-org library is a later ticket).

### Deadlines (CORE-10)

Computed deadlines come from the engine (CORE-09, `docs/06-deadline-engine.md`); manual ones (contractual, internal or court) are entered by people. Persistence planning is pure (`planRecompute` in `libs/deadlines/src/lib/persist.ts`); the screen helpers (`groupDeadlines`, `deadlineMatchesFilters`, `monthCalendar`, `deadlineTraceView`, `nextDeadlineOf`, `confirmationMatches`…) are in `libs/domain/src/lib/deadlines.ts`. ADR-027 records the design.

- **Recompute** (`recomputeMatter`, run by `deadlines-upsertEvent`, `deadlines-deleteEvent`, `deadlines-recompute`, matter creation, `matters-changeStage` and the backfill): one transaction on the matter. It reads its live events (plus a date of death or grant date known only as a key fact), the latest published rule set for its jurisdiction (bundled, or a newer `ruleSets/{id}/versions/*` with `published: true`), its deadlines and pending proposals; computes with the bundled calendars and the org's locality; and writes the plan, bumping `recomputeVersion` when it writes anything (so concurrent recomputes are serialised, and one with nothing to change writes nothing). Statutory rules run only while the version's flag (`deadlines.ruleset.vic.v1`) is on; otherwise they are skipped and their deadlines left alone (`statutoryDisabled`, UI copy `STATUTORY_DISABLED_MESSAGE`). With `templates.system` on, only the matter template's `deadlineRuleIds` apply. Closed and archived matters aren't recomputed.
  - New rule results create `rule-{ruleId}` deadlines, `upcoming` or `blocked` with the rule's reason; `pastOnCreation` marks a date already past (the review queue: `upcoming` and `pastOnCreation`, never marked missed automatically).
  - A date in force never moves: a changed date or a rule that no longer applies to an `upcoming` deadline becomes a `deadlineChanges` proposal (`{deadlineId}-v{recomputeVersion}`) and a `deadline_changed` notification to the responsible lawyer (high priority when a critical date would move into the past). A blocked deadline gets its date directly; unchanged dates take new provenance in place. A rejected proposal is remembered (`declined`) and not made again; pending proposals that no longer hold are `superseded`.
  - A missing calendar year blocks only the rules that need it (`blockedCode: 'calendar_missing'`, an existing date kept) and logs an ERROR ops alert (`alert: 'deadline_calendar_missing'`).
  - Rules with `createsTask` create one task per dedupe key (`tasks/dlt-{key}`, `source: 'rule'`, `deadlineId`, the deadline's `taskId`; counted with `countedOpen: true`) once the matter has reached the rule's stage.
  - A `date_of_death` or `grant_issued` event also sets `deceased.dateOfDeath` or `estate.grantDate`, and fills template tasks waiting on it (`duePending`, CORE-07b).
- **Callables** (names in `DEADLINE_CALLABLES`, inputs in libs/domain; all but `recompute` idempotent):
  - `deadlines-upsertEvent` `{matterId, eventId?, kind, date, time?, label?, sourceDocumentId?, meta?}` (matter.edit; a future date of death is `INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `date`), `deadlines-deleteEvent` `{matterId, eventId}`, `deadlines-recompute` `{matterId}`: return the recompute summary (`created`, `updated`, `proposed`, `superseded`, `calendarMissing`, `createdTaskIds`, `statutoryDisabled`, `recomputeVersion`). Audited as `matter.event_recorded`, `matter.event_deleted`.
  - `deadlines-resolveChange` `{matterId, changeId, accept, confirmation?}` (deadline.override): accepting moves the date (and open linked tasks) or supersedes a removed deadline; a critical change needs the matter number typed (`INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `confirmation`); a decided proposal is `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (`change_not_pending`); a stale one returns `superseded`. Audited as `deadline.change_accepted`/`deadline.change_rejected`.
  - `deadlines-override` `{matterId, deadlineId, dueDate | null, reason}` (deadline.override; `reason` required unless clearing): the override wins; the engine keeps computing, and when its date moves earlier than before and lands before the override the deadline gets `overrideConflict` and the lawyer a notification. Manual deadlines are `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (`manual_deadline`), closed ones `deadline_not_open`. Audited as `deadline.overridden`/`deadline.override_cleared`.
  - `deadlines-complete` `{matterId, deadlineId, outcome: 'done'|'waived'|'missed', completedOn?, reason?, completeLinkedTasks?}`: `done` needs task.edit, `waived` (with a reason) and `missed` deadline.override; `missed` only once the missed check asked (`missedCheck`) or for a past-on-creation deadline (`not_past_due`). Returns the linked tasks still open. Audited as `deadline.done`/`deadline.waived`/`deadline.missed`.
  - `deadlines-proposeRulesetUpgrade` `{cursor?, limit ≤ 500}` (org.settings): recomputes a page of live open matters with cause `ruleset_upgrade` and returns `{processed, upgraded, proposed, created, failed, nextCursor?}`. Audited once per page.
  - `deadlines-acknowledge` `{matterId, deadlineId}` (task.edit; CORE-11): sets `ackBy`/`ackAt` on an upcoming deadline, which stops a critical one escalating; acknowledging again keeps the first. Audited as `deadline.acknowledged`.
- **`deadlines-onWrite`** (trigger on deadlines): keeps `matter.nextDeadline` `{id, dueDate, title, critical}`, the soonest live upcoming deadline with a date (`nextDeadlineOf`), removing it when there is none; and sets the deadline's `nextReminderAt` when its date, criticality, reminder offsets or status changed (Reminders, below).
- **`deadlines-flagMissed`** (daily, 00:15 Melbourne; every MVP org is Victorian): for each org, upcoming deadlines due before today (org time zone), not past on creation and not yet asked about, get `missedCheck` and a `deadline_missed` notification to their assignee. Nothing is marked missed without a person.
- **Client-direct writes.** `task.edit` roles on a live matter they can read create manual deadlines (`ManualDeadlineCreateInput`: `orgId`/`matterId` matching the path, `status: 'upcoming'`, a manual kind, no provenance), edit their whitelist while upcoming (`title`, `dueDate`, `dueTime`, `kind`, `critical`, `assigneeUid`, `reminders`, `ackBy`/`ackAt` as themselves) and soft delete them. On a computed deadline they may only change `assigneeUid` or acknowledge it. Everything else (status, overrides, provenance) goes through the callables. Events and `deadlineChanges` are read-only for clients.
- **Screens** (apps/web `features/deadlines`): `/deadlines` lists the org's upcoming deadlines (`UPCOMING_DEADLINES_QUERY`) in Overdue, This week, Next 30 days and Later, or as a month calendar, filtered by mine, my team's matters, critical, kind and jurisdiction; above them, the proposals waiting on me (`myPendingChangesQuery`, a red banner for `isUrgentChange`) and the review queue (`needsReview`). A matter's Deadlines tab lists its proposals, review queue, open deadlines (a blocked one shows its `blockedReason`), events (recorded, edited and deleted through `deadlines-upsertEvent`/`deadlines-deleteEvent`) and closed deadlines, and adds manual ones. Every row shows its trace (`deadlineTraceView`, also in the DeadlineChip tooltip). The Overview's date of death and grant date key facts are saved as events through `deadlines-upsertEvent` (deleted when cleared), so changing them proposes rather than moves dates in force. Closing a linked deadline after completing its task goes through `deadlines-complete`. Lists say `STATUTORY_DISABLED_MESSAGE` while the matter's rule-set flag is off.

### Reminders and escalation (CORE-11)

Every reminder goes through `notify()` (FND-20). The schedule rules are pure functions in `libs/deadlines/src/lib/reminders.ts`; ADR-031 records the design.

- **Schedule.** A live, upcoming, dated deadline reminds at its `reminders[]` offsets in days. When none are stored, the defaults apply: 30, 14, 7, 3 and 1 for a critical deadline, 7 and 1 otherwise. Each reminder goes out at 08:00 org time on the locality calendar's business day on or before the offset day. `nextReminderAt` holds the next one still ahead. `deadlines-onWrite` sets it when the date, criticality, offsets in force or status change. Reminders already due at that moment (a new deadline, or a date moved earlier) are not sent, and sent ones (`sentAt`) are never resent. `reminders-onOrgWrite` resets every upcoming deadline's schedule when the org's `timezone` or `holidayLocality` changes.
- **`reminders-dispatch`** (every 15 minutes), per org:
  - It sends each due reminder (`deadline_reminder`, keyed `dlrem-{matterId}-{deadlineId}-{offset}`) to the assignee and the responsible lawyer. With no active assignee, it goes to the responsible lawyer and the admins. A critical deadline's reminders are `critical` and always email. Reminders due since the last run collapse into one, marked "Late reminder". Every covered offset gets `sentAt`. A closed matter's reminders are skipped and the schedule moves on.
  - It escalates a critical, unacknowledged, not-yet-past deadline from 08:00 three business days before it is due (`deadline_escalation`, keyed `dlesc-…`). The recipients are the responsible lawyer's `supervisorUid` and every owner and admin, recorded in `escalatedTo` with `escalatedAt`. A moved date clears both, so the new date can escalate again.
  - From 08:00 org time, it reminds the assignee of each open task due within the window, `prefs.notifications.taskReminderDays` days before (default 1, 0–14, rolled back to a business day; `task_reminder`, keyed `taskrem-{taskId}-{dueDate}`). It records `reminderSentFor` (the due date) on the task, so a new due date gets its own reminder.
  - After every org it writes `heartbeats/reminders-dispatch` `{lastRunAt, lastOkAt, finishedAt, orgs, sent, failed}` (server-only) and logs `ok`. The Terraform alert "Reminders not running" (`modules/firebase_env/monitoring.tf`) fires when no `ok` is logged for an hour.
- **Today.** Escalations to me (`myEscalationsQuery`: `escalatedTo` contains me, upcoming) that are not acknowledged appear under Needs attention, with Acknowledge (or `a` on the focused row).
- **Missed deadlines** are `deadlines-flagMissed` (above): asked about, never marked missed by themselves.

### Documents (CORE-12)

Files are uploaded by clients, checked and scanned by `documents-onUpload`, and read through 5-minute signed URLs. The upload contract and helpers are in `libs/domain/src/lib/documents.ts`; ADR-028 records the design.

- **Upload contract.** The client puts the bytes at `orgs/{orgId}/matters/{matterId}/pending/{uploadId}` in the files bucket (`uploadId`: 8–64 of `[A-Za-z0-9_-]`, a UUID in practice; it also becomes the new document's id) with `uploadSettings(...)`: the content type of the extension (`UPLOAD_TYPES`: pdf, docx, doc, xlsx, xls, pptx, msg, eml, txt, csv, png, jpg, jpeg, heic, webp, zip) and custom metadata `uploaderUid` (the signed-in user), `fileName` (with an allow-listed extension), `sha256` (lower-case hex of the bytes), and optionally `documentId` (add a new version of that document), `category` and `privileged: 'true'` (privileged.view roles). At most 50 MB. The Storage rules check all of it; clients can't read, overwrite or delete pending objects.
- **`documents-onUpload`** (Storage trigger on the files bucket, `processUpload`): checks the uploader is still an active `document.upload` member on a live matter they can see (and the version target is live, clean and visible), records the upload on the document (a new document with `scan.status: 'pending'` and `version: 0`, privileged when the matter is; or the target's `upload`), then:
  - **checks** the size, the SHA-256 against the metadata and the type by magic bytes (`detectFileKind`, `kindMatchesExtension`): failures are deleted and recorded as `upload.status: 'rejected'` with a `reason` (`type_mismatch`, `hash_mismatch`, `too_large`…). Corrupt files with the right signature (a damaged PDF, an unreadable Office zip) are stored as uploaded.
  - **scans** through `MalwareScanPort` (ClamAV over HTTP in production, a fake that flags EICAR in the emulator and tests). Infected: the object is deleted, `scan.status: 'infected'` (for a new document) and `upload.status: 'infected'` are recorded, and the uploader and active owners and admins get a high-priority `document_infected` notification through `notify()`, in-app and by email (no file name). Scanner down or slower than 60 s: the file stays quarantined, `upload.status: 'delayed'` ("Scanning delayed"), and `documents-rescan` retries after 1, 2, 4… minutes (at most an hour), 8 attempts in all.
  - **stores** a clean file at `documents/{docId}/v{n}` (`Content-Disposition: attachment`, never overwritten), with `pageCount` (pdf-lib) or `encrypted: true` for password-protected PDFs, adds the version in a transaction (current when newest, `scan.status: 'clean'`, `ingest.status: 'pending'`) and deletes the quarantined object.
  - **dedupes**: for a document's first version, the same transaction reads `documentHashes/{sha256}`; when it names another live document with that content, the new one gets `duplicateOf`, otherwise the index points at the new one. Concurrent uploads of the same file are serialised by the transaction, so exactly one is the original. `documents-resolveDuplicate` keeps (clears the mark) or discards (soft deletes) the copy.
  - Every step is idempotent by upload id (`versionSeq` reserves numbers, kept on `upload.version`), so redelivered events and retries add a version once. A new-document upload whose id names an existing document is refused. Logs carry ids, sizes and outcomes only.
- **`documents-sweepPending`** (hourly): quarantined uploads older than 24 hours are deleted (by generation, so nothing lingers in the versioned bucket) and recorded as `expired`.
- **Versions.** A new version is an upload with `documentId`; history is `versions` (`versionHistory` lists it newest first). `documents-restoreVersion` `{matterId, docId, version}` (document.upload, idempotent) copies an earlier version to a new current one with `restoredFrom`, so history is never rewritten.
- **Downloads.** `documents-getDownloadUrl` `{matterId, docId, version?, rendition?: 'original'\|'previewPdf'\|'previewImage'\|'message'}` (matter.view) returns `{url, expiresAt, fileName, version}`: a V4 signed URL valid for 5 minutes, served as an attachment, through `SignedUrlPort`. Privileged documents and matters are `NOT_FOUND` without privileged.view (readonly and paralegals), removed documents `NOT_FOUND`, documents not scanned clean `FAILED_PRECONDITION` (`not_scanned`), missing renditions `NOT_FOUND` (`rendition_unavailable`).
- **Email.** A stored .eml or .msg version gets a `message` rendition (`renditions/v{n}/message.json`, `MessageRenditionSchema`: subject, from, to, cc, date, plain-text body ≤ 200,000 characters, attachments with `savable`). `documents-saveAttachment` `{matterId, docId, version?, attachmentIndex, category?}` (document.upload, idempotent) writes one attachment to the pending area as an upload by the caller (privileged when the email is, `correspondence` by default), so it is checked, scanned and deduped like any upload; the new document's id is the returned `uploadId`.
- **Bulk.** `documents-bulkUpdate` `{matterId, docIds (≤ 500, unique), change: {op: 'category', category} \| {op: 'delete'} \| {op: 'restore'}}` (document.upload; delete and restore need document.delete), in batches of 250 with `_requestId`; returns `{updated, skipped: [{docId, reason: 'not_found'\|'deleted'\|'not_deleted'\|'unchanged'}]}`. A removed document stays readable and resolves as "Removed document" (`documentDisplayName`).
- **Client-direct writes.** `document.upload` roles edit `name`, `category`, `tags`, `notes` and `accessRestricted` (changing `privileged` needs privileged.view); `document.delete` roles soft delete and restore. Everything else is server-owned. Clients never create documents.
- **Rules.** Firestore: documents are read by members who can see both the document and its matter (privileged ones need privileged.view, so readonly and paralegal lists must query `privileged == false`); `documentHashes` and `chunks` are server-only. Storage: only `create` on `pending/` (as above); stored versions are readable by active members only while the document is clean, live and visible to them; renditions and quarantined or infected objects have no client read.
- **Screens** (apps/web `features/documents`):
  - **Documents tab** (`/matters/{id}/documents`): the matter's documents as a table or a grid (remembered per browser), newest first, sortable by name, category, type, size and date added, and filtered by category, type, privilege (privileged.view roles) and "Show removed" (document.delete roles; a removed document reads "Removed document", with its former name beside it). Roles without privileged.view query `matterDocumentsQuery({privilegedVisible: false})`, so privileged documents never reach them. Rows can be selected to move to a category, remove (after a confirmation) or restore, through `documents-bulkUpdate`, or, for every role, to download as one zip (CORE-12b).
  - **Uploads**: the Upload button or a drop anywhere on the tab adds up to 20 files at a time (`UPLOAD_ACCEPT`; a file off the allow-list, empty or over 50 MB is refused before sending). Each file is hashed (`fileSha256`) and sent with `uploadBytesResumable` (the Storage SDK loads with the first upload), three at a time, with per-file progress, Cancel and Retry (a retry is a new upload id). Once sent, each row follows its document: Scanning…, Scanning delayed, Quarantined, Rejected (the reason in plain words), Ready, or "Duplicate of …" with Keep both and Discard (`documents-resolveDuplicate`). Uploads carry on while you move between the tab and a document.
  - **Document viewer** (`/matters/{id}/documents/{docId}`): the preview beside a details panel. Every byte comes through `documents-getDownloadUrl`. PDFs open in a sandboxed PDF.js viewer: PDF.js is a lazy chunk and its worker a static asset, with no scripting, XFA or `eval`. Pages render to canvas as they scroll into view, with each page's text for screen readers. `?page=N` opens at page N and follows the page in view, so the address is a citation of it. `?v=N` shows an earlier version. Images show inline and plain text (≤ 200,000 characters) as text. Emails show their message rendition (headers, body, attachments) with "Save to matter" (`documents-saveAttachment`). A password-protected or damaged PDF says why it can't be previewed. Office files show their PDF preview and HEIC photos their JPEG preview; until the preview exists (or when the file can't be converted) they say "Preview not available yet; download instead".
  - **Details panel**: name, category, tags and notes (document.upload roles); the privileged switch (privileged.view roles only); versions newest first with Download and Restore (`documents-restoreVersion`) and "Upload new version"; Remove and Restore (document.delete roles); Download of the version shown.
  - Reading the signed URLs with `fetch` (PDF.js, text, email) needs CORS on the files bucket for the app's origins, which Terraform sets (GET and HEAD). The Storage emulator allows any origin.
- **Scanner and previews** (CORE-12a, ADR-032): the ClamAV scan service and the preview service are private Cloud Run services (`apps/doc-services`). After a version is stored, an Office file (`docx`, `doc`, `xlsx`, `xls`, `pptx`) or HEIC photo is queued on `documents-render`, which converts it with the preview service and records `renditions.previewPdf` (`renditions/v{n}/preview.pdf`) or `previewImage` (`preview.jpg`) on the version and, while it is current, the document. A file that can't be converted keeps no preview; an outage retries. Without `CLAMAV_SCAN_URL` uploads stay "Scanning delayed"; without `PREVIEW_SERVICE_URL` there are no previews.
- **Zip downloads** (CORE-12b): `documents-startZip` (`matter.view`, idempotent; up to 200 documents of one live matter, 1 GiB in all) starts a `documents.zip` job (FND-24) with each selected document's current version, named as a single download names it and made unique ("Will (2).pdf"). Removed documents, documents not scanned clean and privileged documents the role can't see are left out and counted (`skipped`); none left is FAILED_PRECONDITION `nothing_to_zip`, too many bytes `too_large`. The job stores (doesn't compress) the files in `orgs/{orgId}/exports/{jobId}.zip`, and the member who started it downloads it through `jobs-getExportUrl` (5-minute signed URL, `application/zip`). A zip is a download, not an export of the matter: it holds only what the member could download one at a time, so it doesn't need `export.includePrivileged`.

### Notes and the activity feed (CORE-13)

Matter notes are Markdown file notes with mentions; the matter's activity feed is a read model of the audit log. Contracts live in `libs/domain/src/lib/notes.ts`; ADR-032 records the design.

- **Mentions.** `@[uid]` mentions a member and `#[matterId]` a matter (`memberMentionToken`, `matterMentionToken`; ids of 1–128 characters without `/`, `[`, `]` or spaces). `parseMentions(bodyMd)` returns each once, in order of first use, ignoring tokens in inline code and fenced blocks; `newlyMentioned(before, after)` gives the uids to notify. The server parses on every save: member mentions of anyone who isn't an active member are dropped (stored nowhere); matter mentions are kept as written, and a reader who can't open the matter sees `RESTRICTED_MATTER_LABEL`.
- **`notes-create`** `{requestId, matterId, bodyMd, pinned?, privileged?}` (`note.write`, idempotent) and **`notes-update`** `{requestId, matterId, noteId, bodyMd, privileged?, expectedUpdatedAt, overwrite?}` (`note.write`, idempotent) both run in one transaction and return `SaveNoteResult {noteId, updatedAt (ISO, the next expectedUpdatedAt), notified[], unreachable[]}`:
  - `bodyMd` is 1–100,000 characters (`INVALID_ARGUMENT` on `bodyMd`: "A note can be at most 100 KB", "Write something first").
  - The matter must be live and visible to the caller (`NOT_FOUND` otherwise). Setting or clearing `privileged` needs `privileged.view` (`PERMISSION_DENIED`, `details.reason: 'privileged_requires_lawyer'`); a note on a privileged matter is always privileged.
  - `notes-update`: a missing, deleted or hidden note is `NOT_FOUND`. Unchanged text and flag is a no-op (no revision, no write). If the note's `updatedAt` (to the millisecond) isn't `expectedUpdatedAt`, it is `FAILED_PRECONDITION` with `details: {reason: 'note_conflict', noteId, updatedAt, updatedBy}` unless `overwrite`. Otherwise the previous text is appended to `revisions/{requestId}` (its `editedAt`/`editedBy`, else `createdAt`/`createdBy`), and the note gets the new text, mentions and flag, `editedAt`/`editedBy` and the usual stamp. Pinning moves `updatedAt` too, so an editor open across a pin sees the conflict.
  - Members mentioned for the first time (never the author) get a `mention` notification through `notify()` in the same transaction (`{noteId, matterId}`, title "Jordan Price mentioned you on RM-2026-0001", a generic body, link `/matters/{matterId}/notes#note-{noteId}`, id `mention-{noteId}-{uid}`, so a retry rewrites it); email and push are enqueued after the commit. A member who can't open the note (privileged, role without `privileged.view`) isn't notified and is returned in `unreachable`.
  - Audited as `note.created` and `note.edited` (counts and flags; never the text).
- **Client-direct writes.** `note.write` roles pin and unpin (`pinned`), soft delete and restore. Text, mentions, the privileged flag and the edit stamp are server-owned; clients never create notes or write revisions.
- **Rules.** Notes are read by members who can see them (privileged ones need privileged.view, so readonly and paralegal lists query `privileged == false`, indexed with `createdAt desc`). A revision is readable when both it and its note are visible: roles without privileged.view list a history with `where('privileged', '==', false)` (indexed with `editedAt desc`); a privileged note's history stays hidden.
- **`activity-onAudit`** (trigger on create of `orgs/{orgId}/audit/{entryId}`) projects audit entries into `matters/{matterId}/activity/audit-{entryId}` through a mapping table (`apps/functions/src/activity/mapping.ts`, `ACTIVITY_RULES`), then moves the matter's `lastActivityAt` forward. Keys are semantic actions or `{collection}.{action}` for writes:
  - matters: `matter.created`, `matter.status_changed`, `matter.stage_changed` (stage title from the template), `matter.template_applied`, `matter.template_upgraded`, `matter.renumbered`, `matter.restored`, `matter.conflict_determined`, `matter.party_added|updated|removed`, `matter.event_recorded`, `matter.event_deleted`;
  - deadlines: `deadline.overridden`, `deadline.override_cleared`, `deadline.done|waived|missed`, `deadline.acknowledged`, `deadline.change_accepted|rejected`, and for manual deadlines only `deadlines.create`, `deadlines.soft_delete`, `deadlines.restore`;
  - documents: `documents.create`, `documents.soft_delete`, `documents.restore`;
  - notes: `note.created`, `note.edited`, `notes.soft_delete`, `notes.restore`, and `notes.update` when only `pinned` changed (`note.pinned`/`note.unpinned`).
  Each line has `kind` (the action, or `document.added`, `deadline.created`, `note.deleted`… for writes), an actor-less `summary` ("Changed the status from Open to On hold", "Added document “Will.pdf”"; the UI puts the member's name first), `actorUid`, `at` (the entry's time) and `refs` (`auditId`, plus `noteId`, `documentId`, `deadlineId`, `partyId` or `eventId`, and a party's `contactId`). Summaries never contain note text; on a privileged matter or for a privileged record they name nothing ("Added a document"). A change recorded both as a callable's write and its semantic event appears once. Entries without a matter, about tasks (`tasks-onWrite` writes those), from the migration, or not in the table are skipped. `activity` is excluded from the audit log and `lastActivityAt` is a noise field, so the trigger can't loop.

### Clerk suggestions (AI-07)

See [ADR-037](adr/037-clerk-suggestions-engine.md). Domain: `libs/domain/src/lib/suggestions.ts`; functions: `apps/functions/src/suggestions/`.

- **Proposing** (server only): `proposeSuggestions({db, orgId, clock, log}, matterId, proposals[], {privileged, promptId, promptVersion, model, ttlDays?})` validates each proposal (`SuggestionProposalSchema`) and skips the ones with these reasons:
  - `invalid`;
  - `duplicate`: the same `dedupeKey` (default `defaultDedupeKey`) as a proposed, stale or dismissed suggestion, or as an earlier proposal in the call;
  - `exists`: the handler finds the record;
  - `matter_unavailable`: a closed or deleted matter;
  - `disabled`: the `clerk.features.suggestions` flag is off.

  Proposed suggestions are written in one transaction with the basis, `privileged` and `expiresAt` (14 days by default). `privileged` is set by the option, a privileged matter, or a privileged cited or targeted document.
- **Kinds with a handler:** task, deadline, event, key_fact, note, party and document_meta. Each applies through the domain logic of its own callable:
  - tasks use `Task`;
  - deadlines use `ManualDeadlineCreateInput`;
  - events use `recordEvent` and so recompute;
  - key facts on events go through `recordEvent`, and the others go through `Matter.update`;
  - notes use `createNote`;
  - parties use `upsertPartyChange`;
  - document details use `MatterDocument.update`.

  time_entry, chronology, order and evidence are refused with `SUGGESTION_UNSUPPORTED`.
- **`suggestions-accept`** `{requestId, matterId, suggestionId, editedPayload?, expectedBasis?}` (`clerk.use` plus the kind's action from `SUGGESTION_KIND_ACTION`, idempotent, flag-gated) applies the suggestion in the domain write's transaction (`TxJoin`) and returns `{suggestionId, status: 'accepted'|'edited', resultRef}`. It refuses with:
  - `NOT_FOUND` when the suggestion is missing, or privileged and the caller lacks `privileged.view`;
  - `ALREADY_RESOLVED` when it is no longer open or is past `expiresAt`;
  - `MATTER_CLOSED`;
  - `PERMISSION_DENIED` (`details.action`);
  - `INVALID_ARGUMENT` (`details.reason: 'edited_payload'`, `details.fields`) for an invalid edit;
  - `SUGGESTION_STALE` (`details.basis`, `details.changed[] {key, label}`) when the basis changed. The suggestion is then marked `stale` with `staleReview`, and accepting again with `expectedBasis` applies it.

  It is audited as `suggestion.accepted` with the human as actor and `data.via: 'clerk'`.
- **`suggestions-acceptMany`** `{requestId, matterId, suggestionIds (1–25)}` accepts each in order. It returns `{results: ({ok: true, suggestionId, status, resultRef} | {ok: false, suggestionId, code, details?})[], accepted, failed}`.
- **`suggestions-dismiss`** `{requestId, matterId, suggestionId, reason?}` (`clerk.use`) sets `dismissed`, `dismissReason`, `resolvedBy` and `resolvedAt`. It is audited as `suggestion.dismissed` (`via: 'clerk'`).
- **`suggestions-expire`** (hourly) marks open suggestions past `expiresAt` as `expired` (`resolvedBy: 'system'`). It uses a collection-group index on `status` and `expiresAt`.
- **Metrics:** outcomes increment `orgs/{orgId}/metrics/clerk-{YYYY-MM}` `values`:
  - `suggestions.{outcome}`;
  - `suggestions.kind.{kind}.{outcome}`;
  - `suggestions.prompt.{promptId}@{promptVersion}.{outcome}`.

  The outcomes are proposed, accepted, edited, dismissed, expired and stale. `suggestionAcceptanceRates(values)` gives the rate (accepted plus edited, over resolved) overall, per kind and per prompt version.

## Invariants (enforced in rules, callables and tests)

1. `members/{uid}` exists and is `active` ⇔ the user's claim includes the org. Claims are synced by the trigger `auth-syncClaims`.
2. Matter `number` is unique per org (case-insensitively). It is allocated only by `matters-createMatter` in a transaction, which also writes its `matterNumbers` reservation.
3. An invoice `number` is gapless and is never reused. A voided invoice keeps its number.
4. A deadline's `dueDate` is never changed silently by a recompute. A recompute writes a *proposal* when the date would move; an `override` always wins.
5. A `timeEntry` with status `billed` is immutable except through a credit-note flow.
6. `audit` and `chunks` cannot be written by clients.
7. `privileged` documents and offers are never readable by `readonly`, and are excluded from exports unless `includePrivileged` is set by owner or admin.
8. Deleting a matter is a soft delete. Hard deletes happen only through retention jobs after `destroyAfter`.

### Today (CORE-14)

The Today screen (`/today`, apps/web `features/today`) is the signed-in person's day in the active org. Its reads are in `libs/data-access/src/lib/today.ts`, the merging and ordering in `libs/domain/src/lib/today.ts`; ADR-029 records the design.

- **The day.** Today is `dateOnlyAt(now, org.timezone)` (`orgs/{orgId}.timezone`, default `Australia/Melbourne`); the week runs to Sunday (`todayWindow`). When the device's wall clock differs from the org's, the header says so (`timeZoneHint`).
- **Needs attention** (live listeners): my overdue deadlines (`myOverdueDeadlinesQuery`: `assigneeUid == me`, `upcoming`, `dueDate < today`) and the proposals waiting on me (`myTodayChangesQuery`). `needsAttention` orders them: changes that would move a critical deadline into the past, escalations awaiting acknowledgement (CORE-11; none yet), overdue deadlines (oldest first, critical first on a day), then the other changes.
- **Due today and this week** (read once): my upcoming deadlines and open tasks due from today to Sunday (`myDeadlinesThisWeekQuery`, `myTasksThisWeekQuery`), merged by `dueThisWeek`: due date, then priority (a critical deadline before everything, any other deadline with high-priority tasks), deadlines before tasks, then time and order. `groupDueItems` heads them Today and Later this week.
- **My tasks** (read once): my next 8 open tasks with a date (`myNextTasksQuery`), most overdue first, linking to `/tasks`.
- **Recent matters** (read once): `users/{uid}/recent` entries of kind `matter` for the org, latest first (`recentMattersQuery`).
- **Numbers** (count aggregation, not while offline): open matters I am responsible for, my deadlines this week, my open tasks; and, for the new-practice checklist (`onboardingSteps`), the org's live matters and active members.
- **Limits and access.** Each section reads at most `TODAY_SECTION_LIMIT` (50) rows and links to its full list when cut. Every query is mine (`assigneeUid == me`) and scoped to the org (`groupRepo`). Each row's matter is read once (`todayMatterRepository().get`); rows on a matter that is refused, missing or deleted are left out without a word.
- **Freshness.** The one-shot reads show this device's cache first (`watchQuery(…, {live: false, cacheFirst: true})`, `getDocsFromCache` then the server) and read again when the window regains focus (at most every 10 s, online only) and after a task changes on the page (`TaskActions.writes`).

### Search and commands (CORE-15)

The ⌘K palette (apps/web `shell/command-palette-dialog.ts`, loaded on the first ⌘K) searches matters, people, tasks and documents and runs commands. The tokeniser and ranking are in `libs/domain/src/lib/search.ts`, the reads in `libs/data-access/src/lib/search.ts`, the providers in apps/web `core/search/search-providers.ts`; ADR-033 records the design (ADR-013 the approach).

- **Tokens.** `search[]` is lower-cased, NFKD-normalised and diacritic-free prefixes of 2–10 characters per word, at most 60 (`searchTokens`). Matters (`matterSearchTokens`): the number first, whole, digits-only, in parts and without leading zeros, then title, client and deceased names and external reference; contacts their names, email local parts, phones and ABN; tasks their title (`taskSearchTokens`); documents their name (`documentSearchTokens`). Server-owned on all four, kept by `matters-denormalise`, `contacts-onWrite`, `tasks-onWrite`/`tasks-onPersonalWrite` and `documents-onWrite` (each writes only a stale `search`, so never loops).
- **Queries.** `parseSearchQuery` picks the lead token (a number's compact form, else the longest word, at most 10 characters); each provider reads at most 20 rows holding it (`array-contains`), most recent first, keeps those matching every word (`matchesSearch`) and ranks exact number, then title prefix, then recency (`rankSearchHits`). One-character queries search commands only. A provider that read its 20 says to type more. Offline, reads come from the device's cache and the palette says so.
- **Access.** Matters add `privileged == false` for roles without privileged.view. Tasks: my own (`assigneeUid == me`, matter and personal) for everyone, and every matter task (`matterId != null`) for privileged.view roles. Documents: the `documents` group for privileged.view roles; others search the open matter's non-privileged documents only. A refused read is an empty group.
- **Recents.** `users/{uid}/recent` entries (`RecentMatter`) carry `kind` (`matter`, `contact`, `task`, `document`), `matterId` and `entityId`; the matter screen, a contact's selection, a document's page and opening any palette result record one. With an empty query the palette lists the latest 10 (`recentEntriesQuery`, `recentTargets`), leaving out what is gone, deleted or no longer readable.
- **Commands.** Global: New matter, New contact, New task (personal), Go to… (each destination), Toggle theme and the shell's. On a matter (the "This matter" group, or Tab on a matter result): Add task, Add deadline, Record event, Upload document, open its tabs, Change stage…, Assign…, Close matter…. On the Matters list: Filter by… and Save view…. A command for another page navigates with `?do=<intent>` (`core/commands/intents.ts`), which that page runs and removes; ⌘↵ opens a result or a page command in a new tab.
- **Backfill.** `materia-scripts backfill-search-tokens --project <id> [--dry-run]` rewrites every `search` with the tokeniser and stamps CORE-14's recent entries `kind: 'matter'`; it pages by document path and writes only what differs, so it is idempotent. `materia-scripts search-load` times the palette's reads on the emulator with 5,000 matters.

### Will safe custody (CORE-17)

The register of original wills, codicils and powers of attorney the firm holds (`/safe-custody`, apps/web `features/safe-custody`). Domain helpers are in `libs/domain/src/lib/safe-custody.ts`, the reads in `libs/data-access/src/lib/safe-custody.ts`, the functions in apps/functions `safe-custody/`; ADR-036 records the design.

- **Writes are callables only.** The rules let members read packets and nobody write them, so every movement carries who, when, why and on what authority. `safeCustody-receive` (`safeCustody.edit`) numbers the packet from `counters/safe-custody` and records an `in` movement; `safeCustody-update` changes items, location, responsible lawyer, will matter and notes of a packet still held or out; `safeCustody-recordMovement` appends `out` (held → out, needs `to`), `returned` (out → held, optionally to a new location), `released` (held or out → released) or `destroyed`. A refused transition is FAILED_PRECONDITION `invalid_movement`, a closed packet `packet_closed`.
- **Release and destruction.** Both need `safeCustody.release` (owner, admin, lawyer), a sign-in within 5 minutes (`REAUTH_REQUIRED` otherwise; the web app asks the person to sign in again and retries) and an `authority`: a document on a matter the caller can open, scanned clean (a signed authority, a death certificate with the executor's ID, a court order). A release also needs `to` or `toContactId`. A destruction only sets `pendingDestruction` and notifies the other owners, admins and lawyers; `safeCustody-confirmDestruction` by a different lawyer records it (`approvedBy`), or anyone with the role declines it (the requester withdraws). Confirming your own is PERMISSION_DENIED `same_person`.
- **Current will.** `currentItemIndex` flags the latest-dated will (or a later codicil). It is a flag only: whether a later will revokes an earlier one is a legal question.
- **Death of a testator.** `safeCustody-onContactWrite` notifies each held or out packet's `responsibleUid` when the testator's `dateOfDeath` is first recorded (`safe_custody` notification, "Original will held: SC-…", no names). The packet page then offers "Open a probate matter" (`/matters/new?type=probate&deceased=…`). A rename refreshes `testatorName` and `search`.
- **Stocktake.** `/safe-custody/stocktake` lists held packets by `locationKey` (site, cabinet, slot, numbers padded). `safeCustody-verify` stamps `lastVerified` on up to 200 held packets at once.
- **PDFs.** `safeCustody-receipt` (the testator's receipt) and `safeCustody-stocktakeReport` (held packets by site and cabinet, with a box to tick) start FND-24 export jobs (`safe-custody-receipt`, `safe-custody-stocktake` templates); `jobs-getExportUrl` gives the member who started the job a 5-minute link once it succeeds. The receipt leaves out the cabinet and slot.

### Notifications (FND-20)

Every notification goes through `notify()` (`apps/functions/src/notifications/notify.ts`); nothing else writes `notifications`. The kind registry, preferences and routing rules are pure and live in `libs/domain/src/lib/notifications.ts`; ADR-030 records the design.

- **Kinds.** `Notification` (schema v2) is a discriminated union on `kind`, each with its own ids: `invite`, `mention`, `assignment`, `task_assigned`, `deadline_reminder`, `deadline_escalation`, `deadline_changed`, `deadline_missed`, `suggestion`, `suggestion_ready`, `conflict_alert`, `invite_accepted`, `document_ready`, `document_infected`, `integration_error`, `export_ready`, `budget_alert`, `safe_custody`, `system`. `NOTIFICATION_KINDS` gives each its settings label, default channels and priority; adding a kind to the union without an entry fails the type check. Mentions, assignments and invites go in-app and by email; `document_infected` (CORE-12) and `safe_custody` (CORE-17) are high and go in-app and by email; `deadline_escalation` is critical; most others are in-app only unless a person turns more on.
- **`notify(ctx, input, {tx?})`.** For each recipient (active members only; others are skipped) it reads `users/{uid}.prefs.notifications`, resolves the channels (`resolveChannels`), writes `orgs/{orgId}/notifications/{id}` (a stable `key` makes it idempotent) and enqueues one `notifications-deliver` Cloud Task per email or push channel. Inside a transaction the writes join it and the caller calls `dispatch()` after commit.
- **Preferences** (`users/{uid}.prefs.notifications`, owner-writable, `NotificationPrefs`): `email` and `push` master switches, `kinds.{kind}.{inapp,email,push}`, `digest` (default on) at `digestHour` (default 7), `quietHours` (default 19:00–07:00; `null` turns them off). Invalid fields fall back to their defaults (`readNotificationPrefs`). **Critical notifications always go in-app and by email**: `resolveChannels` enforces this on the server, and the rules refuse `kinds.deadline_escalation.email: false`.
- **Quiet hours.** Email and push for a normal or high notification created in the recipient's quiet hours wait until they end (`deliverAfter`, the task's `scheduleTime`). The time zone is `users/{uid}.prefs.timezone`, else the org's, else `Australia/Melbourne`. Critical ones are never held.
- **Bursts.** More than `NOTIFICATION_BURST_LIMIT` (100) for one person within a minute collapse: later ones are written with `summaryId` and no channels, and one summary `system` notification (`burst-{uid}-{yyyyMMddHHmm}`, `collapsedCount`) is delivered instead. Critical ones never collapse.
- **Delivery** (`notifications-deliver`, Cloud Tasks, 6 attempts backing off from 30 s to an hour): appends an outcome to `deliveries[]` per attempt. A recipient no longer an active member is `dropped`; one who already read it in-app (unless it is critical), or has no address, an `undeliverable` address or no devices, is `skipped`. Email goes through the MailPort (Postmark, stream `outbound`, templates in `apps/functions/src/mail/templates.ts`; the subject is the notification's `title`, never matter content); with no `POSTMARK_TOKEN` outside the emulator it is `skipped: not_configured`. Push goes through the PushPort (FCM) to each `users/{uid}/devices/{deviceId}`; a token FCM reports unregistered or invalid deletes the device. A transient failure is recorded as `failed` and retried; the last attempt stays `failed`.
- **Digest** (`notifications-digest`, every 15 minutes): for each org and each active member whose `digestHour` has arrived in their local day (unless `digest: false` or `email: false`), emails their deadlines and open tasks due today or overdue and their unread mentions. `digests/digest-{uid}-{date}` (server-only, TTL 30 days) is created first (`create`, which fails if it exists), so a day's digest is sent at most once; a run that fails deletes it to try again next time; with nothing to report it is recorded `empty` and nothing is sent.
- **Bounces** (`notifications-postmarkWebhook`, HTTPS, basic auth `postmark:<POSTMARK_WEBHOOK_SECRET>` checked in constant time; 503 while unconfigured): a hard bounce or spam complaint sets `users/{uid}.emailDeliverability` and sends the person an in-app `system` notification in each of their orgs.
- **In the app.** The shell's `NotificationsStore` listens to my unread in-app notifications (`myUnreadNotificationsQuery`, at most 99 then "99+") for the Inbox badge, and toasts a high or critical one that arrives while online (critical ones stay until dismissed). The inbox itself is CORE-22.
- **Dev.** In the emulator, mail goes to `emulatorOutbox` and push to `emulatorPushOutbox`; `/__dev/mail` (dev builds only) shows what was sent, rendered as Postmark would send it, through the `dev-mail` function, which answers 404 outside the emulator.
- **Writers on `notify()`**: `tasks-onWrite` (`task_assigned`), `conflicts-onPartyAdded`/`onPartyRevived` (`conflict_alert`), deadline recomputes (`deadline_changed`, in the recompute's transaction), `deadlines-flagMissed` (`deadline_missed`) the documents upload pipeline (`document_infected`, keyed `infected-{uploadId}`) and the safe custody register (`safe_custody`: a testator's death, keyed `sc-death-{packetId}`, and a destruction to confirm, keyed `sc-destroy-{packetId}`).
