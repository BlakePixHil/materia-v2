# Terms of service

## Summary

This summary is part of the terms but doesn't replace them.

- These terms are an agreement between your law firm and [Operator legal name], which operates Materia. Your firm's owner accepts them on the firm's behalf. The [Data processing addendum](dpa.md) is part of them.
- **Your firm controls its client information.** We handle it only to provide Materia, on your firm's instructions, and we keep it confidential.
- **Privilege stays with your firm and its clients.** Using Materia doesn't waive it, and we never look at client files except to support you when you ask, or where the law requires.
- Your data is stored in Australia. Email notifications go through a provider in the United States, and Clerk (if your firm turns it on) uses your firm's own AI provider account, outside Australia.
- **Clerk proposes; your practitioners decide.** Your firm stays responsible for the legal work and advice it gives.
- We'll tell your firm about a data breach affecting its information within 72 hours of becoming aware of it.
- You can export your data and leave at any time. When the agreement ends, you have 30 days to take your data, and then we delete it.

## 2. Accounts

2.1 Your firm's owner manages who in your firm has access and with what role. Your firm is responsible for its users, for keeping its users' sign-in details secure, and for removing access when someone leaves.

2.2 Users must be your firm's personnel (or others your firm authorises, such as a locum), and must use their own account.

2.3 Your firm can require two-step sign-in and set session limits. We recommend that it does.

## 3. Your firm's data

3.1 **Ownership.** Your firm owns, or is responsible for, everything it and its users put into Materia, including client information, documents and notes ("your data"). These terms don't transfer any of it to us.

3.2 **Roles.** Your firm is the entity that collects client information and decides how it is used; it is responsible for its own obligations to its clients, including under the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), the Legal Profession Uniform Law (Victoria) and its rules. We handle your data only to provide Materia to your firm and as your firm instructs, as set out in the DPA.

3.3 **Your instructions.** Your firm's use and configuration of Materia (what it enters, who it gives access to, which features and integrations it turns on, and what it exports or deletes) are its instructions to us.

3.4 **What we don't do.** We don't sell your data, use it for advertising, use it to train artificial intelligence models, or use it for any purpose of our own beyond providing and securing Materia.

## 4. Confidentiality and privilege

4.1 We keep your data confidential. Our personnel and contractors can reach it only when needed to provide, support or secure Materia, are bound by confidentiality obligations, and their access is limited and logged.

4.2 We don't access the contents of your client files except at your firm's request (for example, a support request about a particular matter), to investigate a security incident, or where the law requires it.

4.3 Using Materia does not waive legal professional privilege. Materia lets your firm mark privileged material and carries that marking through exports, Clerk and notifications; it never shares privileged material outside your firm without a person at your firm choosing to.

4.4 If we receive a subpoena, warrant or other demand for your data, we will (unless the law prevents us) tell your firm promptly so it can respond, and we will claim privilege on its behalf where it may apply.

## 5. Security

5.1 We maintain the security measures described in the DPA, including Australian data storage, encryption in transit and at rest, separation of each firm's data, role-based access, two-step sign-in, an audit log, malware scanning of uploads and tested backups in a second Australian region.

5.2 We'll notify your firm of a data breach affecting its data as set out in the DPA (within 72 hours of becoming aware of it), and help your firm meet its obligations under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme.

## 6. Clerk and AI features

6.1 Clerk is optional and off until your firm's owner or an administrator turns it on and saves your firm's own API key for the AI provider. Clerk then runs under your firm's own agreement with that provider, which your firm is responsible for. Information sent to the provider is described in the DPA and the Sub-processor list.

6.2 Clerk suggests and drafts; it never sends, files or bills anything itself, and every suggestion waits for a person. AI output can be wrong. Your firm's practitioners remain responsible for checking it and for the legal work and advice your firm gives.

## 7. Deadlines and legal rules

7.1 Materia can calculate deadlines from legal rules. Rules are checked by a practitioner before they are switched on, and each calculated deadline shows the rule it came from. Your firm remains responsible for confirming the deadlines that apply to its matters; Materia is a tool, not a substitute for professional judgement.
